# Positioning: [Your Name], product marketing for technical products

Draft v1, 2026-09-18. This is the source of truth for every page, post and outreach email. Edit it before we write more copy. Anything in [brackets] is a fact only you can confirm.

## 1. What we are positioning

You, as an independent product marketer, to the people who hire one at technical companies: founders and CEOs, heads of marketing, and occasionally heads of product or sales.

Segments in scope: AI and agent infrastructure, developer tools and platforms, QA, testing and observability, cyber security, and horizontal B2B SaaS sold to a technical buyer.

## 2. Competitive alternatives

What a buyer does today if you do not exist. Value is measured against these, not against other freelancers.

| Alternative | Why they choose it | Where it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| Hire a full-time PMM | Permanent, embedded | 3 to 6 months to hire, $150k+ fully loaded, and the first PMM usually gets buried in sales requests before positioning is done |
| Generalist marketing agency | One vendor for everything | Good at ads, design and production; weak on product depth. They ask the founder to write the brief, and the brief is the work |
| Founder or PM does it | Free, they know the product best | Positioning by accident. Feature lists. Launches slip because nobody owns them end to end |
| Fractional CMO | Senior, strategic | Advises, does not execute. Nobody is writing the battlecard |
| Freelance copywriter | Cheap, fast | Words with no positioning underneath. Needs a brief the company cannot write |
| Do nothing | No cost, no effort | The site keeps saying "AI-powered platform for modern teams" and sales keeps explaining the product from scratch |

## 3. Unique attributes

Facts only. No spin.

1. In-house operator experience at a developer-facing test-automation company, covering positioning, launches, competitive research, SEO and AI-search visibility, partner and outreach programs. [Confirm: through a company-wide rebrand and repositioning.]
2. Technical fluency: reads API docs and changelogs, understands CI/CD, test infrastructure, browser automation, agent frameworks and security tooling well enough to interview engineers and get real answers.
3. Executes end to end: positioning, messaging, page copy, launch assets, battlecards, content, outreach. Not an advisor handing over a deck.
4. Framework discipline: April Dunford's positioning method as the spine, so every asset ladders to one story.
5. AI-native workflow: research, competitor monitoring, teardown scans and first drafts run through an AI toolchain. Turnaround in days, not weeks.
6. Built-in search lens: Ahrefs, Search Console and brand-mention programs. Messaging is written to be found as well as read.

## 5. Best-fit customers

Specific enough that we could name five real companies.

- Stage: seed to Series B. Roughly 10 to 100 people.
- Buyer: technical founder or CEO, or the first marketing leader.
- Product: sold to developers, engineering or QA leaders, security teams or IT.
- Situation, one or more: the website does not say what they do; a launch is due next quarter with no owner; sales loses to the same two competitors; content exists but does not rank or get cited; just raised and hiring reps; rebrand or pivot.
- No product marketer, or one who is drowning.

Not a fit: consumer products, agencies, paid-media or social management, enterprises that need a team of twelve, anyone who wants "more content" without a reason.

## 6. Market category

Product marketing for technical products. Fractional or project-based.

Not "marketing agency" (buyers expect production and ads). Not "fractional CMO" (buyers expect strategy without hands). Not "copywriter" (buyers expect words without judgment). The frame: the product marketer you would hire this week if you could.

## 7. Trends (use sparingly)

- AI companies ship weekly and explain rarely. The gap between what a product does and what its site says has never been wider.
- Buyers research in ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity as well as Google. Being cited is the new page one.
- Developer distrust of marketing is at a high. Plain, specific, technical copy is a competitive advantage, not a style choice.
- Product-led and hybrid sales mean the website does more of the selling than the rep does.

## 9. Objections and answers

- "We just need a writer." Then hire a writer. Copy without positioning under it is how sites end up saying "AI-powered platform for modern teams". If positioning is already sharp, I will just write, and it costs less.
- "We are hiring a PMM." Good. I build the foundation and hand it over with a walkthrough. Most first PMMs spend their first quarter doing the Positioning Sprint anyway.
- "You do not know our product." I will after week one. I interview engineers and read the docs. That is faster than teaching an agency what a CI pipeline is.
- "Freelancers disappear." Fixed scope, fixed dates, weekly check-ins, and everything in editable files you own. Judge me on the first sprint.
- "Do you use AI?" Yes, for research, monitoring and first drafts. Interviews, positioning calls and final copy are mine. Generic AI output is exactly what your competitors publish.

## 10. Voice rules

- Plain, punchy, specific. Short sentences. Numbers where allowed.
- Write the way a good engineer explains something to a peer.
- Lead with the buyer's world (their alternative, their frustration), not with services.
- Banned words: leverage, cutting-edge, seamless, unlock, empower, supercharge, best-in-class, world-class, synergy, holistic, robust, next-generation, revolutionary, and "AI-powered" as a differentiator.
- No exclamation marks. No rhetorical questions in headlines.
